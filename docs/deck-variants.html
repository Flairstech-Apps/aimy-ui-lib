Presentations: storytelling on a timeline

Five ways to tell a story as a deck in AiMY's look. Each one tells the same story, the library's own from 27 October to now, so you can compare them on the same words. Every deck works: click it, then use the arrow keys or Space, or press Present to see it full screen.

  • All five share one engine. A fixed 16:9 stage, scaled whole to any screen, so a slide looks the same on a laptop, a projector or a phone. Arrows, Space, Page Up and Down, Home and End move through it.
  • Motion uses the library's tokens only. The camera moves in 500ms (very slow, the longest token we have), lines rise on transitions.dev's text reveal, and nothing dims to show it is past: past and next are said in words and colour.
  • My pick is A, Track, as the library's deck, with B, Spine, as its reading form. Track is the clearest story on a big screen; Spine is the same timeline turned on its side for a deck people read alone. Both can take C's orb horizon for the cover and the close, and B already carries D's "what we learned, what we did" pair.
  • I need your call on two things. Whether 500ms is enough for a camera move across a slide (a longer "scene" token would be new), and which variant, or which mix, goes into the library.
A

Track: one line through the whole story

The timeline runs along the foot of every slide and the camera travels along it. The line fills in AiMY's gradient behind you, and the next moment's date waits at the right edge. Good: the audience always knows where they are and what comes next; it reads as one continuous story. Weak: one idea per slide, so detail goes to the speaker.

B

Spine: the agenda is the timeline

A vertical spine on the left lists every chapter with its state in words: Done, Now, Ahead. The moment reads on the right with what we learned and what we did. Good: holds the most; works as a deck people read on their own after the meeting. Weak: the least cinematic of the five.

C

Horizon: the orb is now

The landing page's orb horizon, as a timeline. The orb rises on the cover, then sits on each moment's mark along the horizon as the story moves left to right. Good: the most AiMY of the five; made for a stage and a big screen. Weak: a headline and a line or two per slide, no more.

D

Two lanes: knowledge to action, drawn

The brand line as a layout. Each moment is a column on one time axis: what we learned above, what we did below, and a line drawn from one to the other. Past columns stay on screen at full strength. Good: every insight visibly comes with its next step, which is AiMY's promise. Weak: every moment needs both halves written.

E

Run: told the way AiMY works

Someone asks; AiMY works through the steps on the left, each ticking done, and each slide is the answer for one step, its words arriving one after another. Good: shows the product's own behaviour while telling the story; strong for demos and updates. Weak: the frame suits progress stories, less so a pitch.
