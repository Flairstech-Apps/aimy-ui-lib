Presentations: a deck for everything else

Horizon and Two lanes tell a story on a timeline. Most decks aren't one: a proposal, a plan, a pitch, a training, an update on a topic. This is the third template, for those. Each variant shows the same deck, a proposal to roll AiMY Knowledge out to Customer Support (mock content). Click a deck, then use the arrow keys or Space, or press Present.

  • Parts, not dates. The talk is split into parts (Why now, What changes, The proof, The decision). Each part opens with a divider, and the agenda is built from them, so the room always knows where it is.
  • Ten kinds of slide, the same in every variant: cover, agenda, part divider, statement, points (three at most, never bullets), figures (two or three, each with what it means), before and after, quote, choice (two options, one recommended), close.
  • The same motion family as the other decks: the mark flies from the cover to the header and back, thinks between slides, words rise in, figures count up.
  • Where the variants differ is how they show where you are: a rail with the agenda (A), a keynote stage with the orb and a bar along the foot (B), or the whole talk as a board the camera moves across (C).
  • When to use which template goes into the presentations skill once you pick: a story with dates is Horizon or Two lanes; anything organised by topic is this one.
A

Chapters: the agenda stays on screen

A glass rail on the left holds the agenda for the whole talk, with the current part lit and a bar per slide inside it; the light glides to each new part. Content sits beside it, left-aligned like a document. For: proposals, plans, trainings and reviews by topic, where people need to follow the structure.

B

Stage: one idea, big, centred

A keynote. Words are large and centred, the orb is the talk's presence (large on the cover and the close, beside each part's title, resting in the corner while you talk), and the whole talk runs along the foot as one bar, a segment per part with its name. For: pitches, kick-offs and talks to a big room.

C

Board: the whole talk on one surface

Every slide sits on one board, a row per part. The camera glides from slide to slide, pulls back to show each part as you enter it, and the agenda is the board seen from above. For: workshops and planning sessions, where seeing how the pieces relate matters as much as each slide.
